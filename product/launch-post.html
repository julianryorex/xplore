The group chat was never built to run a trip. Xplore is.

Today Xplore is out for everyone on iPhone and Android. It keeps a travel group on the same plan, on the same map and in the same photo album, and it plans the trip with you.

The Xplore team21 November 20269 min read

Every group trip has one person holding it together. They own the spreadsheet, the booking emails, the pinned message nobody reads and the answer to “wait, where are we meeting?” Everyone else has a different piece of the plan in a different app.

We built Xplore for that person, and for the five to fifteen people following them. A trip in Xplore is one shared place. The plan, the live map and the photos all know about each other, so a stop on the itinerary is also a pin on the map and an album in the gallery.

What Xplore does

It plans with you, using real places

Tell Scout, our planning agent, who is going and what the trip is for. It asks each traveler three quick questions about pace, budget and food, then drafts the days. Every stop Scout adds comes from a live place search, with opening hours and walking times checked before it reaches your itinerary. Scout cannot add a place it did not find, which is why you will never walk twenty minutes to a restaurant that closed in 2023.

The plan stays editable, by the people running the trip. The organizer can make anyone in the group an admin, and admins can drag a stop, swap a day, or ask Scout to rework Thursday around a late flight. Everyone else sees every change as it happens and has a say through votes and the group wishlist, so nobody rearranges six people's day by accident.

It runs the day

Once the trip starts, the app opens on Today. You see what is happening now, what is next and when to leave. The same information sits on your lock screen as a Live Activity, so most of the group never opens the app at all.

It shows where everyone is

The map shows each traveler as their own photo. Markers fade as a location gets older, so you can tell “here right now” from “here ten minutes ago” at a glance. Tap Regroup and Xplore picks a meeting point that is fair for everyone and sends walking directions to each person.

Location sharing is on only while a trip is active. It switches off by itself when the trip ends, anyone can go invisible for an hour with one tap, and we keep no location history after the recap is made.

It keeps the memories together

Everyone's photos land in one gallery, sorted by day and stop without anyone tagging anything. On the flight home, Xplore turns the trip into a recap: the route, the best photos and the numbers, on a link you can send to people who were not there.

One trip, one place. The plan knows where people are, and the photos know which stop they belong to.

Why it works well

Joining takes one tap

The organizer shares a link. Guests see the full itinerary in the browser before they install anything, and joining needs only a name. A trip is useful only when the whole group is in it, so we removed every step between the invitation and the plan.

It works without signal

Itineraries, tickets and map tiles for the trip area download the night before departure. On a mountain or a subway platform the plan is still there, and edits sync when the phone finds a connection again.

It is easy on the battery

Xplore checks location often when the group is moving or split up, and rarely when everyone is at the same dinner table. A full day of sharing costs about as much battery as an hour of video.

Scout fails safely

When a place search is down or an answer gets cut off, Scout tells you and leaves the plan untouched. It never saves a half-finished day. We test it against outages and bad data before every release, because an agent that edits six people's holiday has to be boring and correct.

Everything else in 1.0

The core is the plan, the map and the gallery. Around it sits the rest of what a group trip needs.

Votes on stops

Scout proposes two or three options, the group votes with a tap, and the winner goes into the plan when the vote closes.

Shared expenses and a trip kitty

Snap a receipt and Xplore splits it by who was at that stop. Pool money up front for shared costs and settle up in one payment each at the end.

Trip wallet

Forward booking emails to your trip's address. Flights, hotels and tickets appear on the right day, readable offline by everyone who needs them.

Save from anywhere

Share a video, a post or a map link into Xplore. It finds the place and adds it to the group wishlist for Scout to plan around.

Split up, meet back

Half the group wants the museum and half wants the beach. Create two tracks for the afternoon and Xplore plans the point where they rejoin.

Disruption watch

Xplore tracks flights, weather and closures. If a delay breaks the first evening, Scout offers a reworked plan before you land.

Book as a group

Reserve a table for eight or six tour tickets from the stop itself. The confirmation goes into the trip wallet.

Arrival moments

Land in a new city and get the local time, the weather, the way to your hotel and what the group is doing first.

Packing and prep lists

A shared checklist built from the destination, the season and the plan. One person brings the adapter instead of four.

Remix a trip

Publish your finished itinerary as a template. Another group can copy it, change the dates and let Scout adapt it.

Watch and car

Next stop and leave-by time on your wrist. Directions to the regroup point in CarPlay and Android Auto for road trips.

Travel profile

Countries, cities, kilometers walked and the people you traveled with most, built up across every trip.

How Xplore makes money

Guests never pay. A group trip has one person who cares enough to organize it, and that person already pays for things on behalf of the group. So Xplore charges the organizer, and the upgrade covers everyone on the trip.

The free plan is a complete product for one small trip. We want a group's first trip to cost nothing, because every free trip introduces five or more new people to Xplore, and some of them organize the next one.

Trip Pass and Plus

Most groups take one large trip a year, and a subscription for that feels wrong. Trip Pass is a single $19.99 payment, about $3 a head for a group of six. Anyone in the group can buy it, which makes it an easy thing to chip in for. Frequent organizers choose Plus.

Bookings

When a group books a tour, a table or a transfer from a stop, the booking partner pays us a commission. Scout chooses stops first and the booking link is attached afterwards, so commission never decides what goes into your plan. Bookable stops are labeled.

Keepsakes

The recap link is free. A printed photo book of the trip, laid out automatically from the gallery and the route, starts at $39 and ships to each traveler who wants one.

Xplore for Hosts

Tour operators, retreat leaders and wedding planners run the same trip again and again with new groups. Hosts pay per traveler and get their own branding, reusable itineraries, a dashboard across all their groups and messages to everyone at once. It is the smallest audience and the one that pays the most per trip.

The kitty and extra storage

Topping up the trip kitty by bank transfer is free and card top-ups carry a small fee. Storage beyond a plan's limit costs $0.99 per GB a month. These two cover their own costs and little more.

What it costs us

Three things cost money per trip: Scout's planning, photo storage and maps. The free plan is sized so that a typical free trip costs us well under a dollar. Scout reuses place research across groups visiting the same city, photos are compressed on the phone before upload, and the map checks location less often when the group is together.

What we will not do

  • Sell or share location data. It is deleted when the trip is over.
  • Show ads, or let a partner pay to appear in a plan.
  • Build a public feed. Trips are private unless you publish one as a template.
  • Charge guests to join a trip someone invited them to.

What comes next

Next on our list are a full web app for planning on a big screen, live translation for menus and signs inside a stop, and car and train legs that Scout can book as part of the plan. Hosts will get payments and sign-up pages, so a retreat can go from announcement to departure inside Xplore.
